Employment Intensity (Employment Access Index)
===============================================

Measure Description
-------------------

Employment Intensity is a gravity-based index that quantifies the accessibility
of jobs from a given geographic location. Higher values indicate greater
proximity to employment opportunities. The measure captures not just the number
of nearby jobs, but the spatial concentration of employment -- a location
surrounded by many jobs at short distances scores much higher than one near
the same number of jobs spread across a large area.

The measure is computed at the Census block group level for all of Virginia,
then aggregated to tract, county, and health district levels.


Formula
-------

For each Virginia Census block group:

    E = sum( p_i / r_i^2 )

Where:
    E    = Employment Intensity for the block group
    p_i  = total number of jobs at location i
    r_i  = distance in miles from the block group center to location i's center,
           clamped to a minimum of 1.0 mile

The inverse-square weighting means a job 1 mile away contributes 1.0 to the
index, a job 10 miles away contributes 0.01, and a job 100 miles away
contributes 0.0001. This rapid decay means nearby employment dominates the
measure.


Hierarchical Distance Approximation
------------------------------------

Computing individual block-to-block-group distances for all blocks nationwide
would be computationally prohibitive. The calculation uses a hierarchical
approximation based on the methodology developed by the Center for
Neighborhood Technology (CNT) for the H+T Affordability Index:

    - Within 34 miles:   Individual Census block job counts and block centroids
    - 34 to 165 miles:   Census tract aggregate job counts and tract centroids
    - 165 to 200 miles:  County aggregate job counts and county centroids
    - Beyond 200 miles:  Excluded (contribution is negligible due to 1/r^2 decay)

The 200-mile cutoff is a simplification from CNT's original 1,256-mile
threshold. Validation against CNT's published values shows a correlation of
0.998 at the tract level, confirming that contributions beyond 200 miles are
effectively zero.

To further optimize computation, a bounding-box pre-filter in latitude/longitude
is applied before computing Haversine distances, reducing the number of
candidate locations evaluated for each block group.


Data Sources
------------

1. LEHD-LODES WAC (Workplace Area Characteristics)
   - Publisher: U.S. Census Bureau, Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics
   - URL: https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/lodes/
   - Version: LODES 8 (2020 Census geography), with LODES 7 fallback
   - File: {state}_wac_S000_JT00_{year}.csv.gz
   - Column used: C000 (total number of jobs per Census block)
   - Geographic key: w_geocode (15-digit Census block FIPS code)
   - Coverage: 13 states within ~200 miles of Virginia:
     VA, MD, DC, WV, NC, PA, DE, NJ, NY, OH, KY, TN, SC
   - Years available: 2002-2023 (pipeline uses 2015-2023)

2. TIGER/Line Shapefiles (2020 Census geography)
   - Publisher: U.S. Census Bureau
   - URL: https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/TIGER2020/
   - Used for: Internal point coordinates (INTPTLAT, INTPTLON) at block,
     block group, tract, and county levels
   - These are not geometric centroids but "internal points" guaranteed to
     fall within the geographic boundary


Distance Calculation
--------------------

Distances are computed using the Haversine formula for great-circle distance
on a sphere (Earth radius = 3,958.8 miles). At Virginia's latitude (~37-39 N),
the error relative to geodesic distance is less than 0.3%, which is negligible
given the 1-mile distance floor and the inverse-square decay.


Aggregation
-----------

The gravity model is computed at the block group level. Higher geographic
levels are derived by averaging:

    - Tract: mean of constituent block group values
    - County: mean of constituent block group values
    - Health District: mean of constituent county values
      (via county-to-health-district crosswalk)


Output
------

Measure name: employment_access_index
Geographic levels: tract, county, health district
Coverage: Virginia (FIPS 51)
Years: 2015-2023 (directly computed, no interpolation or extrapolation)
Value range: approximately 600 to 230,000 (varies by year)
    - Urban areas (NoVA, Richmond, Hampton Roads): typically 15,000-230,000
    - Rural areas: typically 600-5,000


Provenance
----------

This measure was originally sourced from the H+T Affordability Index published
by the Center for Neighborhood Technology (CNT). The H+T Index included
Employment Intensity as one of 17 independent variables in a transportation
cost model. Our pipeline replicates the Employment Intensity calculation
directly from the underlying public data sources (LODES + TIGER), enabling
updates beyond the H+T Index's last release (October 2022, using 2019 ACS data).

The methodology for the gravity model is documented in:
    "H+T Index Methods", Center for Neighborhood Technology, November 2022
    (see method-2022.pdf in this directory)
    Specifically: "Employment Intensity" section, pages 11-12, Equation 3.


Validation
----------

Computed 2020 values were validated against CNT's published emp_gravity values
from the H+T Affordability Index download:

    - Matched tracts: 2,161
    - Pearson correlation: 0.998
    - Mean ratio (computed / H+T): 0.87
    - The ~13% lower values are expected due to the 200-mile radius cutoff
      vs. CNT's national scope; urban tracts show ratios of 0.95-1.00,
      while remote rural tracts show 0.65-0.70 (these benefit most from
      distant employment contributions that our cutoff excludes)
