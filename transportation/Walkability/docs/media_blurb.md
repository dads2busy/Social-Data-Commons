A composite Walkability Index independently computed at block group level by combining LEHD-LODES employment entropy, GTFS transit stop proximity, and EPA street intersection density, then standardized to 2020 Census geographies using area-weighted crosswalks that redistribute pre-2020 values across boundary changes. This annual time series (2017–2023) on consistent geographies enables trend analysis that the EPA's static Smart Location Database does not support. Walkable neighborhoods are associated with lower transportation costs, better health outcomes, and reduced carbon emissions, making this dataset a key input for transportation planning, zoning reform, and community health assessments.
